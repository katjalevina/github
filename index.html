Ekaterina Levina

Department of Linguistics
University of Vienna
Email: ekaterina.levina@univie.ac.at

I am a theoretical linguist working at the interfaces of semantics, pragmatics, and syntax, with a particular interest in linguistic typology and cross-linguistic research. My work investigates how discourse context and lexical meaning shape grammatical form and interpretation.

My current research focuses on reference and anaphora, including definiteness, clitic doubling, and pronominal reference, as well as expletives, discourse coherence, and modality. I also work on lexical semantics and argument realization, particularly external possession, verb alternations, and differential object marking. My research draws on a wide range of languages, including German, Spanish, Russian, Daakie (Oceanic, Vanuatu), Teotitlán Zapotec (Oto-Manguean, Mexico), and Swahili (Bantu).

I am currently a PostDoc at the project Language between redundancy and deficiency. I am affiliated with the subproject “The (non-)deficiency and (non-)redundancy of clitic pronouns” at the Linguistics Department of the University of Vienna.

I received my PhD in Linguistics from the University of Texas at Austin in 2024, following an MA in Linguistics (2014) and a BA in German Linguistics and Spanish (2011) from Humboldt University of Berlin. I also studied German as a Foreign Language at the University of Leipzig and hold a diploma in Law from Saint Petersburg Polytechnic University.
